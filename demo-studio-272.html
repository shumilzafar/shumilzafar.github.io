Why this page exists

The studio's real contact page is broken, and it's the page that turns lookers into bookings. Here's what was wrong, and what this rebuild does about it.

Fault 01, Duplicated page

Everything printed twice

The contact page rendered its full content two times over, the same contact block, the same forms, stacked back to back. The homepage did it to its reviews as well.

Inquiry Form · Subscribe · Contact Information · Make an Appointment · Booking Form · Newsletter, then all of it again Fixed: one of everything, in one place.

Fault 02, Raw form text

The form spilled onto the page

Instead of a working enquiry form, visitors got the form's innards dumped as plain text, the style dropdown read out as a sentence, and nothing on it could actually be filled in.

Tattoo Style Realism American Traditional Japanese Traditional Neo Traditional Portrait Anime Geometric / Dotwork Other / Not Sure Fixed: a real booking form, below, pick a style, send one clean enquiry.

Fault 03, Two sets of hours

The site disagreed with itself

One part of the site said open seven days, 9am to 7pm. The footer said Tuesday to Saturday, 10am to 6pm, closed Sunday and Monday. Both can't be right.

Mon to Sun 9am to 7pm “we operate 7 days a week” vs Tue to Sat 10am to 6pm, Sun & Mon closed Needs one answer from Ged: this page runs the footer hours until he confirms which set is current.

The work, the reviews and the reputation were never the problem, 4.9 stars doesn't happen by accident. The website just wasn't keeping up. Everything on this page is the studio's own words, photos and details, put back together properly.

Before you book

              
The questions everyone asks first, answered the way the studio answers them.

            

          

          

            

              
              
Honestly, yes, it does. Just not as much as people like to make out. After a short while your body's own painkillers (endorphins) kick in and it becomes very manageable.

            

            

              
              
You get what you pay for with tattooing, a good artist won't come cheap, and a cheap one can cost you far more later in cover-ups or laser removal. At Studio 272 all prices are discussed at consultation, so you know exactly where you stand before anything is booked.

            

            

              
              
Tattooing shouldn't be rushed, you're living with the result permanently. As a rough guide, an averagely complex piece about the size of the back of your hand takes around two hours. Larger or more detailed work takes longer, and your artist will give you a realistic idea at consultation.

            

            

              
              
Start with a free consultation. Bring any reference material you like, photos, images, rough ideas, and your artist will work with you to turn it into something personal that won't go out of favour in a few years. Bespoke designs are the studio's thing; recreating a photo or image of your choosing is just as welcome.

            

            

              
              
Eat and drink about an hour before. Wear something that lets you expose the area being tattooed while keeping your dignity, dark clothing is a good idea, just in case of stray ink. Shave the area that morning if you can, and skip the drink beforehand: alcohol thins the blood, and no good tattooist will tattoo you after one anyway.
