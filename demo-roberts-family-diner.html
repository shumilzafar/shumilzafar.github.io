FIRST

What I found on the current site

1.

The menu is three photographs

The Sandwich, Breakfast, and Entrée menus sit on the gallery page as photos of the printed sheets. On a phone that means pinch, zoom, squint, and a photo’s text is hard to search or read aloud, so your dishes are easy for Google to miss.

2.

The homepage says everything twice

The About, the address, the phone number, the opening hours, even the subscribe form, every block on the homepage appears twice, some of it three times. The page is twice as long as it needs to be.

3.

The first pitch is a job application

“Grow Your Vision, want to work for people that care about you? Drop by and grab an application” sits at the top of the homepage, ahead of the food, the hours, and the phone number. Most visitors came to eat, not to apply.

4.

Your own pages disagree with each other

The opening hours say Sunday: Closed, but the breakfast menu photo serves Sunday 10 to 11 a.m. The printed menu also offers prime rib Saturdays from 2 p.m., right when Saturday hours end. A first-time visitor can’t tell which to believe.

Same menu. Thirty seconds faster.

Nothing about the cooking changes. What changes is how fast a hungry person on a phone can answer three questions: what do they serve, are they open, how do I call?

  • Real text, not a photo of text, readable at arm’s length, and Google can read it too, so your dishes can show up in search.
  • Call and directions buttons that stay put while you scroll.
  • Today’s hours up top, with an open/closed note that keeps itself honest.

Everything below this section is that fixed version, filled in with your menu, it’s the page this demo is proposing.

THEN

The menu, readable this time

Breakfast is served till 10:30 a.m. Free refills on Pepsi products, sweet & unsweet tea, lemonade and coffee, it said so on the printed menu all along; now it says so online too.

Friends and family gather here.

Founded in 2015 and family owned and operated ever since, Roberts Family Diner has built its name on bold tastes, “reinventing the way people eat and enjoy food,” in their words, bringing flavors from all over the US to the Sparta area.

Their own summary of the place: “Good Friends, Great Food, and Great times.” That’s the whole business, a local favorite where friends and family gather. The website just has to say so plainly, show the menu in real text, and get out of the way.
